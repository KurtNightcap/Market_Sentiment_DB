Ranks US-listed companies by cash-adjusted leverage (CAL = (debt − cash) ÷ total assets), the takeover-exposure measure from Cremers, Nair and John. Lower = easier and cheaper to acquire. Percentile is measured across the full universe. This is a research queue, not a recommendation. Version 1 uses SEC filings only: no price, market cap, valuation, governance, or ownership data yet.

Read before using: (1) "Unverified debt" means the company reports no standard debt tag; it may truly be debt-free or may use a different tag, so check the 10-Q. (2) Financial-type names are excluded by keyword (banks, insurers, REITs, funds, SPACs), which also removes some legitimate companies; SIC-based filtering is next. (3) Revenue and operating cash flow come from the latest calendar-year annual data, so companies with off-calendar fiscal years may be missing. (4) Low CAL alone is not a thesis. Check why the cash is there, what the business earns, and who could plausibly buy it.
